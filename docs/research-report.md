# Research report — v0.1.0

This report records the disposition of every hypothesis preregistered in
[`research-protocol.md`](research-protocol.md) against the frozen registry at
[`reports/v0.1-lyapunov-registry.json`](../reports/v0.1-lyapunov-registry.json), generated by
`scripts/generate_registry.py` and reproduced byte-for-byte in CI. Nothing here was generated,
inspected, or partially re-run before the protocol was committed.

## Headline finding

**The single preregistered classification threshold did not separate anything in this grid: every one of the 42 cells in both sweeps — including two of the three classical special solutions — was classified "chaotic."** This is reported as the primary finding, not hidden as a failed experiment. It is a real, interesting result once unpacked, and it directly resolves H4 (see below): a naive short-window Lyapunov estimate with a threshold calibrated from limited preliminary exploration does not, by itself, produce the clean stable/chaotic map this project set out to test for. What it *does* show is more interesting than a clean map would have been.

## Integrator validation (prerequisite for trusting anything below)

All three special solutions conserved energy and angular momentum to well within the
preregistered `1e-9` relative tolerance over 5 periods (`tests/test_integrator.py`):
energy drift was `~3e-11` (figure-eight), `~2e-12` (Lagrange), `~1e-12` (Euler collinear).
Angular momentum drift was of the same order. The Lagrange and Euler collinear configurations
returned to within `1e-10` of their initial state after one period, confirming the simulator
reproduces their known exact periodicity. The figure-eight orbit returned to within `1.5e-3`
of its initial state after one period — this is expected and disclosed in the protocol: the
commonly published figure-eight constants carry roughly 8 significant digits, not enough for
machine-precision recurrence, and this residual mismatch matters for interpreting the
figure-eight result below.

Every cell in both sweeps also reports its own conservation diagnostics; the largest observed
energy drift over any full sweep window (t_max = 20 for a generic configuration undergoing
close encounters) was `1.7e-8` relative — three orders of magnitude below the classification
thresholds. **The divergence measured below is not integrator error.**

## Perturbation-magnitude sweep results

Mean estimated maximal Lyapunov exponent (across 3 direction seeds) per base configuration,
at the smallest tested perturbation magnitude (`δ₀ = 1×10⁻⁸`):

| Configuration | Mean λ̂ | Range across all 6 magnitudes | Classification |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Figure-eight | 0.2158 | 0.2099 – 0.2158 | chaotic (barely, and smallest of the six) |
| Lagrange equilateral (equal masses) | 1.1683 | 1.1683 – 1.1683 | chaotic |
| Euler collinear (equal masses) | 1.8594 | 1.8587 – 1.8594 | chaotic |
| generic_a | 0.2787 | 0.2787 – 0.5494 | chaotic |
| generic_b | 0.8006 | 0.7957 – 1.1314 | chaotic |
| generic_c | 0.5659 | 0.5619 – 0.6408 | chaotic |

Every configuration's exponent is essentially flat (constant to 3–4 significant figures) from
`δ₀ = 1×10⁻⁸` through `δ₀ ≈ 4×10⁻⁵`, then begins to rise at the two largest magnitudes
(`6.3×10⁻⁴` and `1×10⁻²`) — the expected signature of leaving the locally linearized
perturbation regime the protocol anticipated. This flatness across four orders of magnitude of
`δ₀` is the evidence the protocol committed to checking, and it supports (without fully
proving — see Limitations) that the small-`δ₀` values are measuring a genuine local rate, not
an artifact of the perturbation size itself.

### H1 — order-of-magnitude separation

**Marginally falsified as literally stated.** The global range across every perturbation-sweep
cell is `0.2099` to `1.8594` — a factor of `8.86`, just under one order of magnitude. H1
required *more* than one order of magnitude between at least one pair. It came close but did
not clear the preregistered bar. Reported exactly as measured, not rounded up.

### H2 — special solutions are more stable than generic configurations

**Confirmed only for the figure-eight orbit; falsified for Lagrange and Euler collinear.** At
matched perturbation magnitude, the figure-eight orbit's exponent (0.2158) is below the median
of the three generic configurations (0.5659). But the Lagrange equilateral exponent (1.1683)
and the Euler collinear exponent (1.8594) are both *larger* than every one of the three generic
configurations — the two "special" solutions are the least stable configurations in the entire
perturbation sweep, not the most.

This is not a bug; it is consistent with established celestial-mechanics theory that this
project did not fully anticipate when H2 was written. **Routh's stability criterion** for the
Lagrange equilateral relative equilibrium requires
`27(m₁m₂ + m₂m₃ + m₃m₁) < (m₁ + m₂ + m₃)²`. For equal masses this is `81m² < 9m²`, which is
false by a wide margin — the equal-mass Lagrange triangle is **analytically linearly unstable**;
stability requires one mass to dominate the other two by roughly a factor of 25 or more (the
regime real Trojan-asteroid systems occupy, not the equal-mass case tested here). Similarly,
the **Euler collinear relative equilibrium is linearly unstable for every mass ratio** — this is
a standard classical result, not specific to equal masses. Both classical facts are consistent
with, and now numerically corroborated by, this sweep. H2's premise ("special solutions are
presumptively more stable") was wrong for two of the three solutions at the mass ratio tested;
the report states this rather than reframing the hypothesis after the fact.

### H3 — monotonic in `|mass_ratio − 1|`

**Falsified.** The exponent is monotonically increasing in the raw ratio `m₂/m₁` across the
entire tested range — not in the *absolute deviation* from 1 that H3 predicted. Concretely:
`|0.75 − 1| = 0.25` is smaller than `|0.5 − 1| = 0.50`, so H3 predicts a *smaller* exponent at
ratio 0.75 than at ratio 0.5. The opposite is observed: `1.0946 > 1.0259`. Every tested
configuration in this sweep sits deep in Routh's unstable region regardless of ratio (the
required ratio for stability is roughly 25:1, far outside the `0.5`–`3.0` range tested), so all
six cells describe degrees of instability within one qualitatively unstable regime rather than
a transition across a stability boundary. This sweep, as designed, was not wide enough in mass
ratio to reach the actual Routh stability boundary — a concrete, disclosed scope limitation for
a future version, not an error in the executed grid.

### H4 — no clean universal threshold

**Confirmed, and this is the headline finding.** All 42 cells across both sweeps — including
the Lagrange and Euler collinear relative equilibria, which are *exact* periodic solutions in
the unperturbed limit — were classified "chaotic" by the preregistered threshold
(`λ̄ ≥ 0.10`). Zero cells fell in "stable" or "borderline." The threshold was calibrated in the
protocol from limited preliminary exploration and, applied to this frozen grid, resolves no
distinction at all. Two explanations are both plausible and are not distinguished by this
version's data (see Limitations): (1) genuinely, within a 3–4 period window, even
analytically-periodic-but-linearly-unstable configurations (Lagrange, Euler) diverge fast
enough to clear a `0.10` cutoff, which is a legitimate and interesting result on its own; and
(2) the unconstrained-perturbation-direction method (below) inflates apparent divergence for
near-integrable configurations. The protocol commits to reporting this outcome rather than
adjusting the threshold post hoc to manufacture a cleaner-looking map, and that commitment is
honored here.

## What the data actually supports

- Two of the three classical special solutions (Lagrange equilateral, Euler collinear) tested
  at equal or near-equal masses are **measurably less stable** than three arbitrary generic
  bound configurations, not more — consistent with, and a numerical corroboration of, Routh's
  classical stability criterion.
- The figure-eight orbit, the one special solution proven linearly stable in the literature, is
  the least divergent of the six base configurations tested here, though it still exceeded this
  study's "chaotic" cutoff — plausibly an artifact of the short window and imprecise published
  initial condition (see Limitations), not evidence that the figure-eight is actually unstable.
- Increasing the Lagrange-family mass ratio away from 1 in the tested direction (`m₂` growing
  relative to `m₁` and `m₃`) monotonically increased the measured exponent across the full
  `0.5`–`3.0` range, but this range never approached the actual Routh stability boundary, so no
  stability *transition* was observed — only degrees of instability within one unstable regime.
- A single scalar Lyapunov-exponent threshold, calibrated from limited exploration, does not by
  itself cleanly separate "looks periodic" from "looks chaotic" in this bounded grid.

## What the data does not support

- A general theory of where the three-body chaos boundary lies. This is one grid, one
  integrator, one threshold convention, and two short integration windows.
- Any claim about the figure-eight orbit's true asymptotic stability — this study's window is
  too short and its perturbation method too coarse (see Limitations) to distinguish "genuinely
  weakly chaotic" from "measurement artifact at the edge of a thin stability region," which is
  exactly the kind of distinction the literature (Simó, Roberts) resolves with rigorous
  monodromy-matrix analysis, not brute-force short-window Lyapunov estimation.
- Any general statement about Lagrange/Euler stability at extreme mass ratios (e.g. Sun–
  Jupiter–Trojan-like systems), since the tested ratio range (`0.5`–`3.0`) never reaches the
  regime (~25:1) where Routh's criterion predicts stability.

## Limitations

1. **Planar, three-body, Newtonian point-mass only** — see the scope boundaries in
   `research-protocol.md`. Out-of-plane perturbations, general N-body dynamics, and
   relativistic effects are entirely out of scope and could change stability conclusions.
2. **Finite integration windows** (`T_max = 20` and `15` time units, ~3–4 periods). These
   windows were shortened during implementation from an original ~10-period design because
   generic scattered configurations can enter close encounters that make the adaptive
   integrator's step size collapse, which was computationally intractable at the original
   grid density within this project's practical time budget. This is disclosed as a real,
   deliberate scope reduction, not hidden. A positive short-window exponent estimate is
   evidence of measured divergence within the window; it is not proof of asymptotic
   exponential chaos, and a small exponent does not prove long-term stability.
3. **Unconstrained perturbation direction.** The Benettin two-trajectory method as implemented
   perturbs the reference state by a random direction in the full 12-dimensional state space,
   without projecting out the directions tangent to conserved quantities (total energy, total
   linear momentum, total angular momentum). A generic perturbation direction therefore
   generally puts the twin trajectory on a slightly different energy shell than the reference.
   For a genuinely chaotic configuration this is a second-order effect. For a configuration
   that is periodic-but-linearly-unstable in the Lyapunov/Poincaré sense (as the Lagrange and
   Euler collinear solutions are here), a nonzero energy offset can itself produce a nearby,
   non-closed orbit whose separation from the reference grows for reasons entangled with — but
   not purely reducible to — the classical linear instability being measured. This project does
   not have a way, within its current scope, to fully separate "genuine sensitive dependence"
   from "this energy-shell artifact" for the Lagrange/Euler results, and states that
   explicitly rather than resolving it. A tangent-space-projected perturbation (constrained to
   the constant-energy, constant-momentum manifold) is the natural v0.2 methodological
   improvement.
4. **The classification threshold is an arbitrary, disclosed convention** (§Classification
   threshold in the protocol), calibrated from limited preliminary exploration rather than
   validated against an external ground truth. This version's central finding is precisely
   that this threshold, applied honestly to the frozen grid, does not resolve a stable/chaotic
   distinction — see H4.
5. **The figure-eight published initial condition has finite precision** (~8 significant
   digits), giving a `1.5e-3` mismatch after one period even before any deliberate
   perturbation is applied. This mismatch is itself an uncontrolled "perturbation" of unknown
   effective magnitude relative to the exact periodic orbit, and likely contributes to why the
   figure-eight's measured exponent, while the smallest of the six, is still positive and
   exceeds the "chaotic" cutoff.
6. **Small sweep** — 6 perturbation magnitudes, 6 mass ratios, 3 direction seeds each. This
   is enough to see the qualitative patterns reported above, not enough for tight statistical
   intervals on the exponents themselves; `std_exponent` in the registry describes only
   variability across 3 perturbation directions at fixed magnitude, not a confidence interval
   on the "true" asymptotic exponent.
7. **Mass-ratio sweep implemented only for the Lagrange family** (see the protocol's scope
   boundary on Euler's quintic and the figure-eight's lack of a mass continuation).
